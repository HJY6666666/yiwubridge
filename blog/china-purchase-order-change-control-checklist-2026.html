China Purchase Order Change Control Checklist: Approve Changes Before Production and QC

Small changes can create large mismatches when a buyer, a China supplier, a printer and a warehouse are all working from different messages. This guide gives overseas buyers a practical way to record, approve and verify changes before cargo moves forward.

Quick answer: every material change, artwork revision, quantity adjustment, timing shift or carton change should have one written record showing the old requirement, the new requirement, impact on price and lead time, evidence to review, and the buyer’s approval. Share the final version with the supplier, QC team and receiving warehouse.

Why purchase-order change control matters

Order details frequently move after an initial quotation: a buyer may adjust a color, add a barcode, reduce a SKU quantity or request stronger cartons. None of these requests is unusual. Risk starts when a change is agreed in a chat message but is absent from the specification, proforma invoice, artwork proof or warehouse receiving list.

Change control is not unnecessary paperwork. It gives each person a reliable version to follow, helps a buyer compare the cost and timing impact, and creates a useful reference when checking finished goods. It does not replace product testing, compliance review or a contract; buyers should obtain specialist advice where those are needed.

Build a simple change log before production

Use one shared spreadsheet, PDF or purchase-order appendix. Assign each request a number and avoid replacing the only copy of an earlier approval. A practical log can be short, as long as it is complete and dated.

Changes that deserve an extra approval checkpoint

Prioritize changes that alter the delivered product or its ability to pass receiving. A material substitution can change durability or appearance. A revised retail box can affect barcode placement and master-carton count. A quantity change can change production capacity, shipment timing and freight planning.

Ask the supplier to state whether production has started, what can still be changed, and whether existing materials or printed components will be used. If the buyer accepts a variation, record whether the old stock may be consumed and which production lots it applies to. For artwork, use a marked “approved for production” file rather than an unlabelled image in chat.

Connect supplier approval to QC and warehouse receiving

Once the buyer approves a change, update the inspection and receiving references. The QC team needs the current product specification, approved sample or photos, packaging details, defect priorities and any test instructions. The warehouse needs SKU names, expected quantities, label format, carton marks, carton count and the buyer-approved instructions for repacking or FBA preparation.

A seven-step buyer workflow

  1. Freeze the starting version: issue the accepted quotation, specification, artwork and packaging requirements together.
  2. Log the request: describe exactly what will change and identify affected SKUs, purchase orders and lots.
  3. Ask for impact: obtain revised price, MOQ, lead time, available evidence and any materials already in use.
  4. Review evidence: check a revised drawing, photo, digital proof or sample against the original requirement.
  5. Approve in writing: state the final decision, effective date and any conditions; do not rely on a verbal agreement.
  6. Distribute the approved version: send it to the supplier, QC contact and warehouse receiving team.
  7. Verify at milestone: confirm the revision at sample, inline check, pre-shipment inspection or warehouse receipt—whichever is relevant.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not approve a change without asking about price and timing. Do not assume the factory will automatically tell the printer, packaging vendor or warehouse. Do not let a new product photo silently replace an approved specification. And do not send a freight booking based on old carton dimensions after packaging has changed.

When choosing suppliers, a consistent change-control response is useful information. It shows whether the supplier can explain impacts, protect the buyer’s approved details and keep production records aligned. Include the topic when using a supplier quotation comparison checklist.

Purchase-order change control FAQ

What supplier changes need written approval?

Approve changes to product materials, dimensions, components, colors, packaging, labels, quantity, price, production timing, carton details and shipping readiness in writing before work continues.

Can a supplier substitute a material after sample approval?

Treat a material substitution as a new approval point. Ask for the reason, revised specification, sample or photos where appropriate, price and lead-time impact, then confirm the decision in writing.

How does change control help pre-shipment inspection?

A current approved specification gives an inspection team a clear reference for checking the correct product version, packaging, labels and carton marks before cargo is released.
