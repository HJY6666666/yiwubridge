China Export Carton Marks and Packing List Checklist

Give every supplier carton a traceable identity, then reconcile quantities, weights and dimensions before consolidation or international shipping.

Quick answer: Issue a carton-mark standard before suppliers pack. Require a unique shipment code, supplier code, SKU, carton sequence and handling marks on each master carton. Before dispatch, reconcile the supplier packing list with carton photos, measured dimensions, gross weight and received warehouse counts. Resolve discrepancies before combining orders or preparing customs and carrier documents.

Why carton identity matters before export

A carton is easy to lose operationally when its label says only a product name or factory nickname. Mixed orders may arrive from several suppliers on different days, while similar cartons contain different SKUs, colors or customer orders. A unique carton ID connects the physical package to the purchase order, packing list, inspection record and final shipment.

Carton marks do not replace commercial invoices, customs data, carrier labels or destination-specific compliance marks. They are the working identity layer that lets the buyer, supplier, warehouse, inspector and freight forwarder discuss the same carton without guessing.

1. Create a carton-mark standard before packing

Send suppliers one written layout instead of accepting a different style from every factory. Include the buyer shipment code, supplier code, purchase-order number, SKU, variant, quantity per carton, carton sequence, country of origin when appropriate, and necessary handling symbols. Keep fonts large enough to read after warehouse stacking and place the main mark on at least two adjacent sides.

Avoid putting retail prices, sensitive customer information or unverified compliance symbols on an export carton. If a marketplace, carrier or destination requires a specific label, manage that label as a separate controlled requirement and confirm the current rule with the relevant platform or logistics provider.

2. Make carton IDs unique and stable

A practical ID can combine the shipment, supplier and sequence, such as YB260902-S03-C007. The same ID should appear on the carton, packing list, warehouse receipt and inspection photos. Do not reuse an ID after a carton is split or repacked; close the old ID and create new child cartons so the audit trail remains clear.

When one carton contains mixed SKUs, mark it as mixed and attach a carton-level content sheet. A total-only packing list is not enough for warehouse checking because it cannot show which carton should contain the missing or damaged units.

3. Build the packing list from carton-level facts

Ask for one row per carton or per identical carton range. Record carton ID, SKU, description, variant, units, net weight, gross weight and outer dimensions. The totals should calculate from those rows rather than being typed separately. Use consistent units and state whether dimensions are in centimeters and weights in kilograms.

Keep commercial descriptions clear enough for document preparation, but do not let a generic invoice description replace the internal SKU. The internal SKU controls warehouse accuracy; the commercial description supports export and import documentation. Both must map to the same goods.

4. Verify dimensions, weight and CBM

Supplier estimates are useful for an early freight budget, but the final quote should use packed-carton measurements. Measure the longest points after sealing, including bulges or protective corners. Calculate cubic volume consistently: for centimeters, CBM equals length × width × height divided by 1,000,000.

Compare total measured CBM and gross weight with the supplier list. Large differences may indicate a packing change, omitted carton or data-entry error. Freight charging rules vary by mode and provider, so ask the forwarder which actual or volumetric basis applies rather than treating the CBM calculation as the final freight charge.

5. Reconcile the warehouse receipt

The warehouse should record arrival date, supplier, carton IDs, received count and visible condition. Photograph marks and damaged packaging before moving or opening the cartons. Compare the received sequence with the supplier's declared range; a list showing cartons 1–10 should not be accepted as complete if carton 6 is absent.

Quarantine unidentified, wet, crushed or opened cartons until ownership and condition are confirmed. If goods require checking, connect the receiving record to the quality-control plan and record any repacking under new carton IDs.

6. Approve consolidation only after discrepancies close

Do not load mixed supplier orders simply because the overall carton count looks right. Reconcile every carton ID, SKU quantity, weight and dimension, then issue a final consolidation packing list. This reduces the chance of shipping another customer's carton, omitting a late delivery or declaring figures copied from an obsolete supplier estimate.

Documents to release to your shipping partner

  • Final carton-level packing list with controlled revision and date.
  • Commercial invoice data mapped to the same SKU quantities.
  • Warehouse receiving and discrepancy-closeout record.
  • Actual weight, dimensions and total CBM summary.
  • Inspection, repacking and carton-condition evidence.
  • Destination, consignee and carrier labeling instructions.

Your freight forwarder and customs adviser should confirm the final document and destination requirements for the product and route.
