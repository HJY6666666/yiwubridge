China Supplier Quotation Comparison Checklist

Turn inconsistent China supplier quotes into one decision-ready comparison before you select a factory or pay a deposit.

Quick answer: Never compare unit prices until every supplier has quoted the same specification, quantity, packaging, trade term and quality requirement. Build one comparison sheet that separates recurring unit cost from tooling, samples, testing, domestic freight, inspection, consolidation and export charges. Mark every assumption and exclusion, verify the payment beneficiary, then compare an estimated landed cost per saleable unit—not the headline factory price alone.

Why the lowest quotation may cost more

Supplier A may quote an EXW unit price in bulk packing while Supplier B includes retail boxes, barcode labels and delivery to a consolidation warehouse. A simple price-column comparison makes Supplier A look cheaper even though the scopes are different. The same distortion happens when one factory excludes tax, samples, molds, color matching, testing or replacement parts.

A quotation is therefore both a price and a statement of scope. Before ranking suppliers, convert every offer into the same commercial basis. This also exposes unanswered questions while they are still inexpensive to resolve.

1. Freeze a common RFQ specification

Send each candidate the same request for quotation. Use a product code and record critical dimensions, materials, grade or composition, colors, surface finish, performance, accessories, marking and packaging. Attach drawings or reference photos when useful, but convert important details into text so the supplier cannot rely on a visual assumption.

Identify which features are mandatory and which may be proposed as alternatives. If a supplier recommends a different material or construction, price it on a separate line. Do not silently mix the alternative into the main comparison.

2. Separate MOQ levels and price breaks

Clarify whether MOQ applies to each SKU, color, design, carton or total order. A quote for 1,000 units across ten colors is not equivalent to a quote requiring 1,000 units per color. Ask for price tiers that match realistic launch and reorder quantities rather than requesting a container price you do not plan to buy.

Record overproduction and underproduction tolerance for customized goods. Also confirm whether the unit price changes when the final assortment shifts. These terms affect inventory investment as much as the nominal MOQ.

3. Make packaging part of the product cost

Specify the unit packaging, protective materials, retail box, inserts, barcode or SKU labels, master-carton strength and units per carton. For private label orders, list artwork, printing plates, color proofs and minimum print quantities separately. The packaging artwork approval checklist helps control files before mass printing.

Request estimated packed dimensions and gross weight for an early logistics model, then replace estimates with actual measurements before shipping. A low unit price can lose its advantage if weak packing creates damage or oversized cartons inflate chargeable volume.

4. Isolate samples, tooling and setup charges

Put non-recurring charges in separate columns: sample fees, courier cost, molds, dies, printing plates, setup, certification support and custom fixtures. State who owns a tool, where it is stored, how long it is retained and whether any fee is refundable after an order.

A tooling fee should not be blended into a unit price without a clear amortization quantity. Keep the underlying charge visible so later reorders can be checked on the correct basis.

5. Quote measurable quality requirements

A promise of “good quality” cannot support acceptance. Define dimensions and tolerances, appearance limits, function tests, packaging checks and any buyer-required compliance evidence. State whether inspection will occur during production, before shipment or at warehouse receipt, and agree how confirmed defects will be reworked, replaced or credited.

Use the YiwuBridge quality-control service and the pre-shipment inspection checklist to translate the approved specification into checkable evidence. Inspection cost belongs in the sourcing budget even when it is paid to a third party.

6. Normalize trade terms and logistics

Record the agreed Incoterm and named place exactly as quoted. Do not compare an EXW factory price with a price that includes domestic delivery or export handling as if they were equal. Ask which party handles pickup, export clearance, documents and delivery to the port, warehouse or forwarder.

For mixed-supplier orders, add domestic freight, warehouse receiving, checking, storage, repacking and consolidation. The warehouse consolidation guide explains why carton measurements and shipment timing affect the final logistics plan.

7. Compare estimated landed cost per saleable unit

Build a planning total using product cost, packaging, one-time charges allocated across the planned quantity, domestic transport, inspection, consolidation, export freight, insurance, duties, taxes and destination delivery where known. Divide by expected saleable units, allowing for agreed spare parts, test samples or a realistic defect reserve.

This estimate is not a customs or freight guarantee. Duties, taxes, carrier charges and exchange rates depend on product classification, destination and timing. Label provisional inputs and ask the relevant customs broker or logistics provider to confirm them before commitment.

8. Verify supplier identity and payment terms

Match the quotation company, contract party, business evidence, invoice issuer and bank beneficiary. Investigate unexplained requests to pay a personal or unrelated account. Confirm deposit percentage, balance-payment trigger, currency, bank fees and what evidence is required before the balance is released.

For marketplace sourcing, follow the 1688 supplier verification checklist or the Taobao wholesale supplier guide. A strong price does not compensate for an unclear counterparty.

Use a weighted decision, not price alone

After normalization, score candidates on specification fit, evidence quality, communication, sample result, lead time, capacity, quality remedy, payment risk and total estimated cost. Document why the preferred supplier won and which risks still require controls. For an unfamiliar factory or product, a smaller pilot order may produce better evidence than negotiating the last fraction of unit price.
