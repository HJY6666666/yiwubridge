How to Verify a 1688 Supplier Before Paying a Deposit

A low listing price is not supplier due diligence. Verify who will make the product, who will receive payment, and what happens if quality or delivery fails.

Quick answer: Match the 1688 shop to a legal company, document any different payment beneficiary, approve a physical sample and signed specification, define QC and remedy terms, and test the workflow with a small order. Treat platform badges and chat promises as leads for verification—not proof.

1. Reconcile the legal and payment identity

Record the shop URL, displayed company name, business licence details, registered address, contact and beneficiary. Ask for a written explanation when the beneficiary differs from the licensed entity. A licence confirms that an entity exists; it does not prove production capability or ownership of every listed product. For meaningful orders, use a supplier audit or local visit.

2. Make the quotation auditable

Attach the exact listing or drawing, model, material, dimensions, color tolerance, logo process, packaging, quantity, unit price, tooling, sample fee, domestic freight, lead time and tax treatment. State which specification controls if the listing changes later.

3. Turn the sample into a specification

Approve a clearly identified physical or pre-production sample. Photograph measurements, finish, function and packaging, then assign a reference number. Add measurable tolerances because an approved sample alone can still create disputes during mass production.

4. Define QC before production

Agree when inspection happens, who pays for rework and what evidence is required before balance payment. Check quantity, dimensions, appearance, function, labels, barcodes, packaging, carton marks and transit protection. See the YiwuBridge quality control workflow.

5. Control payment and shipping handoffs

Use a small pilot order for a new supplier or specification. Keep deposit, milestone and remedy terms in the purchase record. Confirm whether goods go to your China warehouse, freight forwarder or export agent, and who provides commercial documents. For mixed 1688 orders, calculate carton dimensions and actual CBM before choosing freight.
